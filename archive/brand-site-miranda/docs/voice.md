# Pace & Publish — Voice

## Audience

Fans who follow several motorsport series and want all the news in one place, without checking multiple apps and news sources. They usually read us either first thing after sleeping through a race or practice session, or after work when they want a quick update on the day.

## Personality

- **Authentic.** Every caption is written in our own words. It is violated by copying or lightly rewording another outlet's article or caption.
- **Professional.** We report what happened without taking sides. It is violated by giving an opinion on a race outcome or showing a favorite team or driver.
- **Intriguing.** Each caption leads with a fact that makes the fan want to open the post. It is violated by a caption that just repeats the facts the fan will read in the article anyway.

## We say / We never say

| We say | We never say |
|---|---|
| "Lando Norris takes P1 in FP2, with Oscar Piastri P2 and Fernando Alonso P3." | "Lando dominated FP2, as usual." |
| "It's race week and we're headed to Montreal." | "We think this weekend belongs to McLaren." |
| "There are some rumors around the paddock about next season's line-up. Nothing is confirmed yet." | "We suspect a big driver move is coming next season." |
| "Aramco Aston Martin F1 Team" (the current, full team name) | An outdated team name, such as "Racing Point" |
| "The sprint race results are in." | "That sprint was a shitty mess." |

## Two example captions

**On-voice:**
> It's race week and we're headed to Montreal. The weekend will be packed with a sprint race, F1 Academy, and F2.

Why it works: it states the location and the series racing that weekend as facts and gives no opinion on any team or driver.

**Off-voice:**
> We have some feelings about the French…

Why it's wrong: it gives an opinion instead of reporting news, and it is negative about a group of people.

## Hard rules

- Never state a rumor as fact. Mentioning a rumor is acceptable only if the caption clearly labels it as a rumor, for example "some rumors around the paddock."
- Never predict the outcome of a race, a championship, or a season.
- Never use outdated information about teams, drivers, or series. Check that names, line-ups, and schedules are current before a caption goes out.
- Never swear.
- Never be negative about a nationality or any other group of people.
- Never give an opinion on a race outcome and never show a favorite team or driver. This includes phrases like "we think" and "we suspect."

## Voice checklist

A reviewer answers yes or no to each question. Every answer must be yes before a caption is approved.

1. Is every statement a confirmed fact, and is every rumor clearly labelled as a rumor?
2. Is the caption free of opinions, predictions, and favorite teams or drivers, including phrases like "we think" and "we suspect"?
3. Are all team names, driver names, and series details current?
4. Is the caption written in our own words rather than copied from the source article or another account?
5. Does the caption lead with a fact that gives the fan a reason to open the post, rather than repeating the whole article, and is it free of swearing?
